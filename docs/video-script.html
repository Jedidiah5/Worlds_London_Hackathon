The Loop House

Two minutes, voiceover over screen capture. The one idea the whole film has to land: this is not a video of a place, it is a place being generated while someone walks through it. Everything else is support.

Capture this first

Everything below is cut from screen recordings of the app plus one phone clip of the real corridor. Get all of it before editing, because a second capture session means another live model run.

  • Record system audio, not just the mic. The footsteps, room tone and key chime are generated inside the page. A normal silent screen capture will lose all of it and the film will be half as good.
  • One unbroken take of four loops — roughly four minutes. This is where the reset and the decay come from, and the loops have to be consecutive to be honest.
  • A clean search: walk up to furniture, press E, hold on the hands opening the drawer.
  • A key find and a full escape: the chime, the brass objective banner, the walk to the lit doorway, the white-out, the end card. Let the end card sit for three seconds before you stop recording.
  • Phone clip of the actual corridor at the venue, shot from standing height looking toward the lit doorway — same framing as the anchor photo. This is the shot that proves the trick.
  • Browser full screen, no bookmarks bar, no tabs. Debug panel off, settings closed.

Script

Timecodes are targets, not gospel. Brass lines are read aloud; sage lines are what the viewer is looking at.

0:00 Shot 01 10 sec

Cold open

On screen

Black, three seconds, room tone already running. First generated frame fades up: the green corridor, hanging ferns, the lit doorway at the far end. HUD reads LOOP 01 · 09:00 · 01:00.

Voiceover

This is the building we are standing in. Not a photograph of it. Not a 3D model of it. It is being generated, frame by frame, right now.

0:10 Shot 02 15 sec

The anchor

On screen

Split screen. Left: the phone photo of the real corridor, held still. Right: live generated frames of the same corridor, moving. Hold the split long enough that the viewer matches the two.

Voiceover

We gave a world model one photograph of this floor. Everything you are about to see is derived from that single image, live. There are no 3D assets. Nothing was rendered in advance.

0:25 Shot 03 20 sec

It answers to you

On screen

Screen capture with a small keystroke overlay in the corner. Walking the corridor, then a mouse pan across the seating booths. Footsteps clearly audible. Cut to a search: hands come into frame and open a drawer.

Voiceover

You walk it. The camera answers the mouse. The floor answers your feet. Reach out, and your own hands come into frame.

Beat — hold two seconds on the hands with no narration. Let the drawer sound carry it.

0:45 Shot 04 22 sec

The reset the whole film

On screen

The clock ticking down: 00:03, 00:02, 00:01 — stay on the HUD. Cut to black. The card: You wake up. Again. Then LOOP 02. The corridor returns: same geometry, same doorway, but dimmer, ambers gone cold, the ferns visibly longer.

Voiceover

Every sixty seconds, you wake up back at the start. Same building. Same seed. Same photograph.

Beat — four full seconds of silence across the black card and the return. Do not talk over the reset. This is the shot the whole entry rests on.

Voiceover

That was nine in the morning. This is after hours.

1:07 Shot 05 20 sec

It decays

On screen

Fast cuts, three or four seconds each: LOOP 03 with the strip lights stuttering; LOOP 04 overgrown, the corridor choked with growth. Let the room tone darken with them — it is already mixed to.

Voiceover

The lights fail. The plants take the building. By the fourth loop you are walking through something that used to be an office.

Voiceover

A video model hands you a clip that already decided all of this. A world model re-derives the same place, live — and lets it rot.

1:27 Shot 06 16 sec

The key

On screen

The drawer opens, the brass key catches the light, the chime lands, a hand lifts it. Cut to the brass objective banner appearing across the bottom of the screen.

Voiceover

There is a key. You will not find it on your first loop. And if you are too slow, the loop takes it back off you.

1:43 Shot 07 14 sec

Out

On screen

Walking the length of the corridor toward the lit doorway. The door opens, white floods in, the frame blows out to pure white, and the cream end card lands: You left the building with the loop count and time inside.

Voiceover

Find it, reach the door, and you get out.

Beat — no narration over the white-out or the end card. Let the wash of sound do it, and hold the card for three seconds.

1:57 Shot 08 13 sec

Tag

On screen

End card holds, then the title: THE LOOP HOUSE. Underneath, small: built on Reactor LingBot World 2 · Worlds London 2026.

Voiceover

The model owns the world. Our code owns the truth — the timer, the key, and what you remember. That split is why it runs.

Delivery

  • Burn in captions. Assume a judge watches it muted the first time. The VO lines above are the caption text.
  • 1080p, MP4, under 2:10. Keep the app audio under the voiceover rather than replacing it.
  • No music bed unless it is very quiet. The room tone and footsteps are doing real work and a track will bury them.

Never on camera

  • The debug panel. Backtick toggles it. Check it is off before recording, not after.
  • The settings panel. Useful live, wrong on film.
  • Any error or queue screen — "Every GPU is busy", "Too many sessions this minute". True, handled, and not the story.
  • A loop where the world drifts badly. It happens after long fast walking. Reshoot rather than explain it.
Beats matter more than words here. The three silences — on the hands, across the reset, and over the white-out — are what make it feel like a place rather than a demo. If something has to go to hit the runtime, cut narration before you cut a beat.
